Research

Process

I wanted a checkered floor. The glue made the paper wet and it started to lift and ripple. I’ll come up with a solution, or a way to use the effect later.

I waited for the PVA glue to dry, then used sandpaper to flatten the raised surface, and it started to look like a marble floor!

I wanted at least one or two things in the diorama to be almost hyperrealistic, albeit miniature. The rest of the scene would be rather abstract, so the realistic elements ground the piece as a whole: they give the viewer a point of reference, an anchor, so they know what they’re looking at.

This is the painting, the artist’s mind coming to life. The folded paper structures are inspired by apartment blocks, but they are intentionally not fully realised or painted in detail.

The viewer should resolve the structure in their own way.

The colours of the paints largely mirror the colours on the floor of the abstract part of the diorama, which represents the artist’s mind and the painting coming to life.

I found this little piece of wood in the box of wood. It was the only unrefined piece, and I was inspired by its organic forms: I felt my diorama was missing just that. I picked some leaves outside and attached them to the wood to make a mini tree.

I joined the pieces with bendy metal wire, because it felt more in line with the focus on materiality and tactility.
