Reflection + Evaluation

When I finished the presentation, there was one question: “How does the card make you feel?” What a fantastic question. Anyone who knows even a little about tarot knows that a strong personal reaction to a card can matter more than whatever “definite” meaning has been assigned to it by an external/official source.

I had spent so much of the week researching British Roman history and mythology, making sure I could tie together all the conceptual threads of the final piece; I researched the composition of traditional tarot cards, the proportions of the human skull, symbolism, historical references. Everything needed to be tied together in a neat intellectual bow. But I had never stopped to ask myself the question: how do I actually feel about what I’m making? How does all of this make me feel?

I thought it was a brilliant question, and I later thanked Jonathan for asking it. I tend to lean on my intellect a bit too much? After this assignment I promised myself to be a bit more impulsive, instinctive with the next few projects.

Despite having lived in several different countries, I have almost always lived near the sea, and I continue to gravitate toward it. So beneath all of the research and analysis, there was clearly already an emotional force drawing me toward this theme. Perhaps the research did not produce the meaning of the work so much as give structure and language to something unconscious within me.
